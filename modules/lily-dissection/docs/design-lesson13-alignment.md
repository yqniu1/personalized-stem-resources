# Lesson alignment

Lily is an independent supplement informed by [OpenSciEd](https://openscied.org/) Grade 4 Unit 4.4, Lesson 13. The lesson uses flower investigation and student models to examine structures that support reproduction, building toward NGSS 4-LS1-1.

The resource gives learners another representation to discuss alongside the lesson's investigation. It focuses on structure and function: where pollen is made, where it arrives, and how the structures contribute to reproduction.

## Design choices

| Goal or constraint | Module choice | Review question |
|---|---|---|
| Explain structures working together | Function descriptions and an animated pollen transfer | Can students explain the relationship, beyond completing actions? |
| Keep the lesson goal prominent | Part names are supporting vocabulary | Does visible copy drift toward a naming quiz? |
| Support exploration | Separate and reassemble structures | Does the separation help students observe the relevant structures? |
| Provide guidance | Task sequence and a visible pollen target | Do students understand the action without excessive teacher help? |
| Fit classroom distribution | A standalone offline HTML file | Does it work on the devices teachers actually use? |

Coherence, scaffolding, and explanatory feedback are examples of learning-pattern guidance used to reason about these choices. They are not independently tested mechanisms in this prototype.

## Representation limits

The public 2-D view shows one bloom, with pollen moving to that bloom's stigma. The earlier private 3-D view shows transfer between blooms. The single-bloom illustration should not be described as visually modeling transfer between flowers.

Seed appearance is a simplified outcome cue. Pollination and subsequent seed formation involve additional processes, and successful reproduction is not guaranteed by pollen landing on a stigma. Lilies also have petal-like sepals; the illustration simplifies their functions for discussion.

The mission checks actions, not students' arguments or models. Use separate student explanations and work to examine the intended learning. This supplement has not undergone a classroom learning-effect study.
